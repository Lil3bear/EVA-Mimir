# Terminology and Authorial Voice

Operational extension of the micro writing Skill, inspired by Ding Xiaohan (丁霄汉), https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/639732057. The naming rubric below is our adaptation, not a quotation or an attributed naming theory.

## Choose Names by the Distinction They Carry

For each new term ask: what scientific distinction must the reader remember, what actual mechanism supports it, and can the term be understood after a short definition? A literal inventory such as three-layer memory reports a count, but does not explain why prepared calls, observations and provenance are separated. Consider a concise purpose-led name, then define its concrete records. Do not rename a conventional component merely to manufacture novelty.

Compare at most three alternatives using meaning, ease of reading, reuse across sections and risk of overclaim. Prefer two or three ordinary words to a long noun stack. Do not invent an acronym unless it genuinely reduces repeated burden. A term sounding clever is insufficient: reject names implying semantic verification, environmental restoration, guaranteed transfer or automatic composition when those mechanisms are not established.

For this manuscript, evaluate names such as evidence memory or traceable memory against the actual journal/map/ledger interface; these are possibilities, not mandatory replacements or established terms. Preserve the useful implementation names inside the definition. If a term's existing use in the literature has not been checked, say so in the audit ledger, not in the manuscript. Apply a chosen term consistently to abstract, introduction, method, captions and conclusion only where they refer to the same concept; do not rename related but distinct objects.

## Write the Paper, Not the Editing Diary

Explain the proposed method affirmatively: purpose, inputs, operation, output and evaluation. Remove editorial self-commentary about what this draft fails to describe or which reviewer might object. Preserve scientifically consequential limitations in the appropriate methods/limitations passage; do not hide them, but do not repeat the same disclaimer in every paragraph.

Distinguish three meanings: a verified mechanism, an intended design, and an empirical observation. Make the status explicit where necessary without apologies, sales language or defensive qualifiers in every sentence. Do not use a fluent story to turn a design into an implemented result.

The author's style preferences are concise technical English, restrained quotations and code typography, no decorative long dashes or coined hyphen chains. Mathematical subtraction, legitimate identifiers and necessary field-standard compounds are not forbidden. Replace a phrase only if the new wording improves comprehension without erasing scope, units or attribution.

## Deliver a Naming Ledger

Record original term, chosen term or decision to retain, one-sentence rationale, supporting implementation/paper passage, all affected locations and literature-status uncertainty. Separate a lexical improvement from a new scientific claim. A successful rename does not close an implementation or experiment comment.
