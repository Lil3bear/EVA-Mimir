# Paper Readability

When reviewing new names or overly defensive/self-referential prose, read [terminology and authorial voice](references/terminology-and-voice.md). Choose concise, mechanism-faithful names, not ornamental synonyms or invented novelty. Keep the naming decision and evidence uncertainty in a separate ledger.

For actual passage revision, read [the local repair protocol](references/local-repair-protocol.md). It connects the four diagnostic axes to minimal edits, result reporting and a meaning-preservation check. Use only the relevant cases, not a quota of edits.

This revision checked the user-supplied full article, not a newly fetched webpage. The four source-inspired axes correspond to 逻辑强度、防御弹性、迷惑时间、信息密度. Read [paragraph diagnostics](references/paragraph-diagnostics.md) for concrete tests and [author style](references/author-style.md) for this author's preferences. The existing [passage audit](references/passage-audit.md) provides a compact per-passage record; these worksheets are independent extensions.

This Skill owns local clarity and reasoning. It supports **writing** but does not decide novelty, verify external citations or invent missing **math** definitions. Classify a proposed change by the axis it repairs, preserve the scientific comparison and check the revised passage against neighboring text. Hand missing evidence to **claims/review**, not to a thesaurus.

Check four separate questions rather than replacing words with more elaborate synonyms:

1. Logical strength: does the stated causal, contrastive or sequential connection actually hold? Resolve vague pronouns and distinguish structural changes, training differences and evaluation differences before drawing a comparison. A connective cannot supply missing reasoning.
2. Defensibility: which source, table, artifact or observation supports each material claim? Distinguish a demonstrated effect from a hypothesis or indirect observation. “May” and “suggests” cannot rescue a claim without a basis. Do not add citations to create an impression of authority without checking relevance.
3. Comprehension time: explain an unfamiliar component near its first use, identify its inputs and role, and remove noun stacks and ambiguous references. Retain necessary local definitions instead of sending readers repeatedly to distant sections.
4. Information density: remove irrelevant setup, repeated conclusions and implementation inventories that obscure the scientific message. Keep units, denominators, uncertainty, comparison groups and scope. A percentage-point difference is not a relative percentage gain.

Check captions and nearby analysis for clear comparisons and defined abbreviations. Source inspection cannot establish final figure legibility, clipping or pagination; require a separate PDF check for those claims.

Use the author's short, direct style. Do not introduce decorative dashes, coined hyphen chains, unnecessary quotation marks or code typography. Preserve established technical terms, literal identifiers when needed, mathematical signs and valid LaTeX syntax. Check every changed passage against the original meaning; leave clear prose unchanged rather than forcing a rewrite.

Return only supported issues with exact location, original wording and a minimal remedy. Deduplicate existing comments. Separate language repairs from missing experiments or unverified references; do not mark a scientific concern complete merely because its wording improved.
