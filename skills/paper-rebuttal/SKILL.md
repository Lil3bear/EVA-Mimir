# Paper Rebuttal

During a terminology or writing audit, test whether a reader's strongest objection concerns an awkward name or an unsupported mechanism. A clearer name can fix the former, not the latter. Report the evidence-backed defense and remaining requirement separately; do not write a simulated rebuttal into the paper's normal prose.

## Choose the Mode

For material objections, read [the counterevidence and defense playbook](references/counterevidence-defense.md). Use its evidence grades, closure tests and answer templates in both simulated review and actual response preparation. The protocol is an independent extension of the attributed principles.

- Simulated review: test the strongest legitimate defense before retaining an objection. Return supported, partial, rebutted or evidence-missing judgments. Do not fabricate real reviewers, scores or a submitted rebuttal.
- Authorized manuscript revision: use the concern ledger to determine whether wording, a verified clarification or additional evidence is needed. Apply supported text changes through **writing**, with actual change status; preserve unresolved scientific requirements.
- Actual rebuttal preparation: read the supplied full reviews and applicable response rules, then draft answer-first responses tied to exact evidence and concrete changes. Prepare a confidential chair note only when explicitly requested and allowed.

## Context and Evidence

Read the current paper, complete supplied reviews, existing responses and available experiment artifacts. Identify target venue/year/track, response limits and permitted new evidence from supplied official rules. If those rules are unavailable, mark them unverified; do not assume a confidential AC channel exists. Do not contact reviewers, submit a response, or upload private material without the author's explicit instruction.

Build a response ledger: exact reviewer ID and concern -> fair interpretation -> paper location/counterevidence -> position -> response -> concrete change -> evidence/change status. Distinguish actual conference reviews from simulated AI criticism. Never invent reviewer scores, agreement, quotations, experiments or completed edits.

## Response Strategy

- Write for both reviewers and the decision-maker: make the scientific issue and its resolution understandable without asking the chair to reconstruct a long dispute. Prioritize concerns that affect validity, contribution or the decision; combine genuine duplicates while retaining every reviewer/concern identifier.
- For a valid criticism, acknowledge the specific issue and give the correction or evidence. For a factual misunderstanding, state the disagreement calmly and point to exact text, protocol or results. For a reasonable but out-of-scope request, explain the task boundary and what it would or would not establish. Do not accept a false premise merely to sound polite, or reject criticism merely because it is inconvenient.
- Explain why a requested dataset or baseline is relevant or not; other papers omitting it is not sufficient justification. Cite representative work only after checking its applicability. Do not generalize the author's anecdotes about reviewers, chair competence or score changes into facts about the present review.
- Where supplied reviews genuinely disagree, identify the precise scientific disagreement and resolve it with evidence. Attribute supportive comments accurately and preserve their caveats; praise or another reviewer's vote is not a substitute for evidence. Do not cherry-pick quotations, manufacture consensus or provoke personal conflict.
- Optional confidential chair note: only for a material, readily demonstrable factual or procedural concern. Give a short exact quotation, the conflicting evidence and its decision relevance. Check any claimed policy violation against actual rules. Ask that the evidence be considered, not that a reviewer be punished or a score discarded. Do not speculate about competence, identity or motives; ordinary scientific disagreement is not misconduct.

## Concrete Changes, Not Empty Promises

For each accepted change state what changes, where, and its status. Supply revised wording, a precise figure/table adjustment or supported results where available. A section reorganization should identify moved content, the new overview and subsection roles, without promising an unspecified rewrite.

Use distinct statuses: proposed / implemented in source / compiled / verified, or experiment not run / run with available evidence. Results not yet obtained remain pending; never phrase them as completed. Check page/word limits and dependencies when the actual policy is supplied. Do not insert placeholder numbers or guarantee future experimental gains.

## Writing and Final Audit

Answer the issue first, then evidence and any bounded change. Be firm, specific and respectful; neither mandatory apologies nor a prescribed phrase such as “we beg to differ” is required. Avoid personal accusations, sarcasm and claims that the chair must accept the paper.

Check every reviewer attribution, numerical comparison, source reference, new claim and change status. Ensure the response and current manuscript agree. When performing simulated peer review, use this skill to test the author's strongest legitimate defense; do not invent an actual rebuttal or relax a valid concern without evidence. In review-only mode, return concerns and possible evidence-backed defenses, not manuscript patches.

Deliver the requested response draft, a compact concern/evidence/change ledger and unresolved blockers. Keep a confidential-chair draft separate from the public response when explicitly requested and supported by the venue. Report missing evidence and unchecked rules. Never mark paper comments resolved simply because a response was drafted.
